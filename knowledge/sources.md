## City of Boulder — City Manager's 2027 Recommended Budget (at a glance)
Link: https://bouldercolorado.gov/budget-glance

- Total 2027 recommended budget: about $552.6M, up about 6% from 2026. Operating budget about $417.2M (up 2.3%); capital budget about $135.4M (up about 19.5%).
- General Fund: $200.5M, up 3.09%. The city closed a $6.3M General Fund gap. This is the third straight year of reductions, starting in 2025, driven by rising costs and flat sales, use and property tax revenue.
- Cost drivers include a 5% raise for police and fire union members, 4% for BMEA members and merit increases for non-union staff.
- Staffing: 24 positions eliminated (13 filled, meaning layoffs, and 11 vacant). Of 27 term-limited positions, 12 extended, 3 converted to ongoing, 12 ending. 8.5 vacant positions frozen until 2028. The city expects reduced service levels.
- Homelessness and housing: one-year (not ongoing) funding for local housing vouchers and the Flexible Resolution Program, which help people exit homelessness; one-year funding to continue the Day Services Center; ongoing funding for increased eviction prevention through rental assistance; a Housing Project Planner converted from term-limited to ongoing; a vehicle for the mobile Community Court.
- Sugar-Sweetened Beverage Tax revenue now pays for the Youth Services Initiative, EXPAND and REquity, leaving less for grants and new initiatives.
- Police: photo radar van program eliminated; three police lieutenant positions added under the 2023 Reimagine Policing Plan; one-time funding continues the police Employee Assistance Program.
- Parks: Limited Commission Rangers move from Parks to the Open Space and Mountain Parks ranger program in 2027. The page does not mention encampment enforcement budgets.
- Council schedule: study session Sept. 10, 2026; public hearings Oct. 1 and Oct. 15, 2026.

## City of Boulder — 2026 City Manager's Budget Message (Aug. 29, 2025)
Link: https://stories.opengov.com/cityofboulderco/published/XZZic7sLm

- 2026 General Fund cut 7.8% versus 2025, capped at $194.48M, closing a forecast $7.5M gap. Three filled and 16 vacant positions eliminated.
- The budget aligns city services with the updated Homelessness Strategy, which centers the partnership with All Roads (formerly Boulder Shelter for the Homeless). All Roads is to focus on in-shelter navigation.
- Funding for BTHERE (Boulder Targeted Homeless Engagement & Referral Effort) cut by $115,000 to reduce duplication with other nonprofits.
- $196,000 in grant funds offset some direct city reductions, to build diversion and rapid rehousing capacity.
- Municipal Court case management for clients without active cases ended (saving $99,000).
- The $48,000 Bridge House contract for Ready to Work graduate housing support was eliminated; Bridge House invited to apply for competitive human services funding instead.
- Building Home Program (peer support for newly housed people) increased by $275,000 from a one-time balance in the Eviction Prevention and Rental Assistance Services Fund.
- Safe and Managed Public Spaces (encampment response) staffing reduced by two vacancies, saving $205,000; Urban Park Rangers continue.
- Crisis response: the message described combining CIRT (clinician with officer) and CARE (clinician with paramedic); the combined Behavioral Health Crisis Response team began operating in the third quarter of 2025. CIRT responded to 2,274 calls in 2024, up 60% from 2023; CARE averaged one or fewer behavioral health calls a day. Two paramedic positions and one vacant clinical case manager eliminated; $483,000 realigned.
- $100,000 of one-time pandemic-era Human Services Fund grant money not continued.

## Boulder Reporting Lab — Boulder sets goal to end unsheltered homelessness by 2028 (Aug. 14, 2025)
Link: https://boulderreportinglab.org/2025/08/14/boulder-sets-goal-to-end-unsheltered-homelessness-by-2028/

- The city's updated strategy sets a goal to end unsheltered homelessness as soon as 2028. Officials estimated it would take about $11M more per year for services and housing, which appeared unlikely given budget pressures.
- July 2025 point-in-time count: at least 140 people sleeping outside.
- The Safe and Managed Public Spaces program, which clears encampments, costs about $4M per year.
- Camping ban enforcement increased near downtown and the Boulder Creek path. Police were working with the city attorney on approaches for people who refuse to leave, possibly including location bans in municipal court sentences.
- The police chief acknowledged a severe lack of backup places when the shelter is full.
- All Roads has about 180 winter beds. The strategy adds no shelter beds; it aims to shorten average stays from 73 to 46 days.
- The city's only youth shelter had recently closed, citing funding.
- Plan elements: more diversion, prioritizing by length of homelessness, more permanent supportive and affordable housing, short-term rental subsidies, lower-cost housing types, partnering with county and state on residential treatment, centralizing food and supply distribution at the Day Services Center.

## KUNC — Boulder County cuts millions in safety net funding (Oct. 16, 2024)
Link: https://www.kunc.org/news/2024-10-16/boulder-county-cuts-millions-in-safety-net-funding-as-demand-for-services-rises

- The county's Community Partnership Grant program gave about $12.3M to nonprofits in 2024 and planned about $7.9M for 2025, a 36% cut, citing falling state and federal funding, the end of pandemic stimulus and a projected deficit.
- All Roads received about $900,000 in 2024 and expected a 30% cut, possibly reducing beds despite rising demand.
- EFAA (rental assistance) expected a 20% cut after its highest-ever client volume, as eviction filings rose.
- Rocky Mountain Equality (formerly Out Boulder County) expected a 15% cut, likely reducing therapy for clients on public insurance or uninsured.
- City councilmembers were concerned and some were caught off guard; one said the county had failed to be a good partner. Council agreed to write to the commissioners.

## City of Boulder — High Utilizers Initiative
Link: https://bouldercolorado.gov/projects/high-utilizers-initiative

- "High utilizers" are a small share of unhoused people who cycle repeatedly through police, prosecution, courts, jail and emergency care, often with serious mental illness or substance use disorders, usually for low-level chronic offenses.
- Partners: City of Boulder (coordinator), All Roads, AMR, Boulder Community Health, Boulder County including the Sheriff, Clinica Family Health & Wellness, Colorado Community Health Alliance, Focus Reentry, Metro Denver Homeless Initiative, and the 20th Judicial District including the District Attorney's Office.
- Cost: up to about $67,000 per person per year in criminal justice costs; over $84,000 including emergency and hospital care. The city estimates $48,000–$84,000 per typical high utilizer.
- In 2022 about 20% of criminal justice high utilizers were also high utilizers of the Boulder Community Health emergency department; nearly all were unsheltered and did not regularly stay in shelters.
- Proposed pilot: scattered-site housing with intensive voluntary wraparound services (health, behavioral health, peer support, case management, retention support), based on the FUSE model. Phase one estimated at $1.7M for units, rent subsidies, a damage assurance fund and housing case management, assuming wraparound services come from existing resources.
- Fundraising is paused pending the city's Homelessness System Evaluation. The city has contributed staff time and part of a consultant's cost, with no other direct city or county funding.
- Gaps named: more permanent supportive housing; criminal histories block leasing even with vouchers; conventional supportive housing often excludes high utilizers; better data sharing; more housing-focused mental health and addiction treatment, which depends on state and federal money.
- Reported locally (not in this source, to verify): high utilizers were placed into existing All Roads permanent supportive housing without added services, straining staff and units and hurting the program's reputation.

## Homeless Solutions for Boulder County — Executive Board packet (Nov. 2024)
Link: https://assets.bouldercounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Homeless-Solutions-for-Boulder-County-Executive-Board-Packet-11.2024.pdf

- The City of Boulder flagged the end of federal ARPA money and urged planning. HSBC has limited funds and cannot subsidize other agencies' programs; reductions would likely apply across all programs.
- Agenda listed budget impacts on rapid rehousing, coordinated entry and decreased shelter capacity.
- Coordinated entry screenings were rising quickly. Housing exits were flat in September 2024, mainly because vouchers for Mental Health Partners were frozen.
- Through September 2024: Boulder exited 134 people from homelessness and Longmont 39; 1,484 people exited since 2020.
- About $1M in Emergency Solutions Grant funding was requested for the housing hotline, shelters and street outreach.

## Boulder County — A new approach to housing
Link: https://bouldercounty.gov/news/a-new-approach-to-housing/

- The county created a Housing Department bringing together housing and homelessness programs from Housing and Human Services and Community Services, with the Boulder County Housing Authority, reporting to a new position. Human Services now focuses on human services only.
- 71% of voters approved the 2023 Affordable and Attainable Housing tax, effective January 2025. Over 28,000 county households are housing cost-burdened.

## Colorado Sun — Clinica and Mental Health Partners merger (June 14, 2024)
Link: https://coloradosun.com/2024/06/14/clinica-merger-mental-health-partners/

- Clinica Family Health and Mental Health Partners merged into Clinica Family Health & Wellness, combining physical, behavioral and oral health care in Boulder, Broomfield, Gilpin and Adams counties.
- Reasons given: more complete care, financial sustainability amid Medicaid and Medicare pressures, revenue diversification and recruiting. Clinica served about 59,000 patients a year at eight sites.

## Boulder County — Human Services Fund and community partnership grants
Links: https://bouldercounty.gov/government/your-taxes-at-work/human-services-funding/ and https://bouldercounty.gov/?p=195150

- County grants have funded Boulder Shelter for the Homeless (All Roads), Bridge House, Bridge to Justice, Boulder Housing Partners, Mother House (now Haven Ridge), Clinica and Mental Health Partners, among others.
- Mental Health Partners received about $3.25M for general operations, withdrawal management and community-based services in the 2025–26 grant cycle.

## Other context
- TGTHR closed Chase House, the county's only residential program for homeless youth, after the earlier closure of The Source youth shelter (Boulder Reporting Lab, 2025).
- Federal McKinney-Vento funding for students experiencing homelessness faced proposed consolidation and cuts in the FY2026 federal budget proposal (NPR, June 2025).
- Haven Ridge is the new name of Mother House.

## Oversight records and evaluations (documented)
- Public Policy Associates evaluated Homeless Solutions for Boulder County (completed Jan. 2025, released April 2025; the city paid $75,000). It found "a lack of cohesion" among Boulder County, City of Boulder and Longmont policymakers, recommended reviewing governance to clarify roles and accountability, and found the local data system isn't connected to the Metro Denver Homeless Initiative system. Council members said the report lacked quantitative depth. Sources: https://assets.bouldercounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Findings-from-the-System-Evaluation-of-Homeless-Solutions-for-Boulder-County.pdf and https://boulderreportinglab.org/2025/02/13/report-highlights-gaps-in-boulders-homelessness-response-as-city-refines-strategy/
- Clutch Consulting Group helped update the city's homelessness strategy in 2025 (camping-ban enforcement, moving food distribution to All Roads, priority for people with local ties). The strategy names no published performance measures or reporting schedule.
- Feb. 2024: some city council members said they lacked information on program effectiveness to set funding priorities as federal stimulus money ended. https://boulderreportinglab.org/2024/02/09/newly-created-city-of-boulder-homelessness-programs-are-running-out-of-money/
- Camping ban lawsuit: the ACLU of Colorado sued in 2022 (Feet Forward v. City of Boulder). Dismissed Dec. 2024; the Colorado Court of Appeals affirmed on May 14, 2026 (2026 COA 37).
- April 2025: the Colorado Department of Human Services suspended TGTHR's The Source and Chase House after an audit cited incomplete background checks, missing training records and staff in roles they weren't qualified for. The state cleared The Source to reopen May 9; TGTHR closed it May 13, citing funding.
- 2021: the ACLU wrote that HSBC's six-month residency requirement for shelter was unconstitutional; HSBC reversed it. In May 2026 All Roads began a pilot limiting stays to 10 days for people without local ties.
- Bluebird, a supportive housing site operated by All Roads that opened Jan. 2024, had 362 police calls in its first year, many involving overdoses; All Roads said that is typical for a new site.
- No ethics complaints, conflict-of-interest findings or investigations involving city or county homelessness funding, the county housing authority, All Roads, Clinica/Mental Health Partners, Bridge House or EFAA were found for 2020–2026.
