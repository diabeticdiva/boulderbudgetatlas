About the atlas

Boulder Budget Atlas maps the 2027 budgets of every agency and nonprofit working on homelessness in Boulder County and shows where one budget undercuts or duplicates another, with an AI assistant that answers from public sources.

This is an early version, not a finished analysis. Dollar figures come from the City of Boulder’s 2027 recommended budget, Boulder County budget documents and nonprofits’ public tax filings. Each figure is labeled with its year and source, and figures from different years are never added together. Where a program’s spending isn’t published, the atlas says so instead of estimating.

Reading the map
PlatformsEach platform is a category of work, like shelter or justice. Organizations stand on the category where most of their related spending sits.
ShapesEach shape is an organization. Its height reflects its total budget. The shape shows its type: boxes for the city, cylinders for the county, pyramids for courts and prosecutors, a cone for the school district, stacked discs for housing authorities, hexagonal columns for nonprofits and an octahedron for philanthropy. An outlined shape means the total isn’t published.
LinesEach line is an issue between two budgets. Thickness shows the budget at risk: for a cannibalizing issue, the published spending of the program being undercut; for a redundant issue, the smaller of the two overlapping lines. A dashed line means that amount isn’t published. Arrows point from the spending that undercuts to the program it undercuts.
HeatHeat reflects how many issues touch a category, weighted by type and confidence. Cannibalizing issues count fully and redundant ones count half.
How to read an issue
CannibalizingOne entity’s spending works against another entity’s goal, and often against its own.
RedundantTwo budgets pay for the same work without sharing it.
DocumentedThe facts behind the issue are stated in a budget, plan, meeting record or news report.
PotentialA pattern inferred from how the programs work. It has not been confirmed with the entities or their data.
ReportedRaised by people with local knowledge. Parts may be documented; the pattern itself still needs confirmation.
Oversight and accountability

Each issue names the elected or appointed bodies responsible for overseeing the spending involved, and the gaps where no single body is accountable. The atlas lists a concern about conduct only when a public record documents it, such as an audit, a lawsuit or a regulator’s finding. An issue is a coordination problem, not an accusation of bad faith.

The assistant

“Ask the atlas” answers questions across every organization and issue, using the atlas and summaries of public sources. It can be wrong. Check anything important against the original documents.
